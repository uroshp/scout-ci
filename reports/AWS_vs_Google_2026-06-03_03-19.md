# Competitive Intelligence Brief: AWS vs. Google Cloud
---

## Executive Summary

**1. Google Cloud's acceleration is structural, not a blip — and it is now growing faster than AWS in percentage terms.**


Among the major cloud providers, Amazon maintains a strong lead in the market, though Microsoft and Google continue to achieve substantially higher growth rates. Their Q1 worldwide market shares were 28%, 21%, and 14% respectively, per Synergy Research Group.
 The growth gap is the real story: 
Google Cloud brought in \$20.03 billion in revenue for Q1 2026, up 63% year over year, with operating income rising to \$6.6 billion from \$2.2 billion in the prior-year period.
 AWS, by contrast, 
grew 28% to \$37.6 billion, its fastest pace in 15 quarters.
 Both are accelerating — but Google is accelerating harder. 
Cloud accelerated again due to strong demand for AI products and infrastructure; revenue grew 63%, exceeding \$20 billion for the first time, and the backlog nearly doubled quarter-on-quarter to over \$460 billion.


**So what:** AWS has a massive absolute-revenue lead, but Google is closing the gap at speed. A rep who walks into a 2026 enterprise deal treating Google as a non-threat is flying blind. Every deal in AI, data analytics, and multicloud must be treated as contested.

---

**2. Google's \$32B Wiz acquisition is the most dangerous competitive move in years — it hands Google a foot in the door at AWS accounts.**


Google has officially acquired Israeli cybersecurity firm Wiz for \$32 billion in all-cash, a full year after the companies announced the deal. This marks Google's biggest acquisition in its history.
 
Wiz is trusted by 50% of the Fortune 100, including Shell, BMW, LVMH, Morgan Stanley, and Salesforce, among others.
 The explicit strategic intent is multicloud encroachment: 
the transaction represents a significant investment by Google Cloud to accelerate two major trends in the AI era: improved cloud security and customers' ability to use multiple clouds.
 Critically, 
Wiz products will continue to work and be available across all major clouds, including AWS, Google Cloud Platform, Microsoft Azure, and Oracle Cloud.


**So what:** Wiz's multicloud positioning is the Trojan horse. Google now owns a security vendor already embedded in AWS production environments at half of the Fortune 100. AWS reps must immediately map which accounts have active Wiz relationships, then lead with AWS's native, deeply integrated security stack (GuardDuty, Security Hub, Macie, IAM Access Analyzer) and the strategic risk of letting a competitor cloud own your security vendor's roadmap.

---

**3. Anthropic is AWS's primary AI partner — and that is now a documented, contractual fact, not marketing language.**


Anthropic continues to choose AWS as its primary training and cloud provider for mission-critical workloads.
 
Over 100,000 customers now run Claude on Amazon Bedrock. Together, AWS and Anthropic launched Project Rainier, one of the largest compute clusters in the world, and currently use over one million Trainium2 chips to train and serve Claude.
 In April 2026, Anthropic committed to spend 
more than \$100 billion over the next ten years on AWS technologies.
 While Anthropic also uses Google TPUs, 
Amazon remains Anthropic's primary cloud provider and training partner.


**So what:** When customers say "Google has Anthropic," the correct response is: Anthropic chose AWS as its primary provider and signed a \$100B commitment. Over 100,000 organizations run Claude on Bedrock today. Google is a secondary compute supplier for Anthropic — not its home.

---

**4. Google's "product graveyard" reputation is a durable, exploitable trust deficit — especially in regulated enterprise deals.**


The cumulative effect of so many product shutdowns has created a measurable credibility problem for Google. Google has discontinued over 200 products throughout its history, from Google Reader to Hangouts to Glass. This pattern of launching and killing services has created a persistent trust deficit that affects how consumers and enterprises evaluate the company's new offerings.
 This concern is not limited to consumer products: 
this skepticism is not limited to tech enthusiasts on forums. Enterprise customers evaluating Google Cloud and Workspace offerings have raised concerns about the company's commitment to long-term support, and competitors have used Google's track record as a selling point for their own platforms.
 Contractually, 
Google can discontinue any GCP service with 12 months' notice.


**So what:** In regulated-industry deals — financial services, healthcare, government — where switching costs are existential, raise this proactively. AWS's track record of keeping services running (S3 has been available since 2006 and never been deprecated) is a concrete differentiator. Twelve months' notice to migrate a production platform is not enterprise-grade commitment.

---

**5. AWS is reaccelerating and has more AI deal momentum than is being reported.**


AWS segment revenue climbed 28% year-over-year to \$37.6 billion in Q1 2026, the fastest growth rate in over three years, reaching an annualized run rate of \$150 billion.
 
AWS's AI revenue run rate is over \$15 billion.
 Beyond Anthropic, 
OpenAI expanded an existing \$38 billion AWS commitment by \$100 billion over eight years, with Amazon planning to invest \$50 billion in OpenAI.


**So what:** The narrative that Google is "winning AI" is incomplete. AWS just landed the two largest AI lab commitments in history — from both Anthropic and OpenAI — in the same quarter. Lead with this in any deal where AI infrastructure credibility is in question.

## Competitive Battlecard

### 🟢 WHERE AWS WINS

**1. Anthropic and OpenAI: both primary AI partnerships sit with AWS**

This is the most important fact in the AI infrastructure battle. 
Anthropic continues to choose AWS as its primary training and cloud provider for mission-critical workloads.
 
OpenAI also expanded an existing \$38 billion AWS commitment by \$100 billion over eight years.
 
Over 100,000 customers run Anthropic Claude models on AWS.


**Soundbite:** *"The two most important AI labs in the world — Anthropic and OpenAI — both chose AWS as their primary cloud. That's not a coincidence. That's a vote on who has the infrastructure, the support, and the custom silicon to build frontier AI at scale."*

**2. Breadth of services and marketplace depth**


The AWS Marketplace serves customers with 42,240 products and services, including 11,478 infrastructure software solutions.
 Google Cloud has no comparable marketplace. AWS also offers a far wider managed database portfolio — RDS, DynamoDB, Aurora, ElastiCache, Neptune, Keyspaces, DocumentDB, and MemoryDB — against GCP's more limited options.

**Soundbite:** *"Google has great AI and great analytics. But if you're running production workloads that touch 15 different services, GCP doesn't have the service depth. You'd be stitching together Google and partners for half your stack."*

**3. Agentic AI for the enterprise — Bedrock AgentCore**


Following the general availability of Amazon Bedrock AgentCore on October 13, 2025, AWS partners are building production solutions. AgentCore provides the foundation to build, deploy, and operate agents using any framework or model without infrastructure management, offering services for Runtime, Memory, Gateway, Identity, and Observability.
 Unlike Google's Vertex AI, which is tightly integrated with Gemini, 
AgentCore provides a runtime, gateway, memory, identity management, policy engine, code interpreter, browser tool, evaluations, and observability. It works with any framework (LangChain, CrewAI, custom) and any model, not just Bedrock-hosted models.


**4. Model-neutral AI via Bedrock**


Amazon Bedrock gives access to hundreds of foundation models from leading AI companies along with evaluation tools to pick the best model based on unique performance and cost needs.
 
Bedrock powers generative AI for more than 100,000 organizations worldwide, including startups to global enterprises across every industry.
 Google's Vertex AI is deeply integrated with Gemini — switching models means switching paradigms.

**Soundbite:** *"Google wants you to run Gemini on Google Cloud. We want you to run whichever model is best for your workload — Claude, Llama, Nova, Mistral — on the infrastructure where your data already lives."*

**5. Platform longevity**


Google can discontinue any GCP service with 12 months' notice.
 
Google has discontinued over 200 products throughout its history. This pattern of launching and killing services has created a persistent trust deficit that affects how enterprises evaluate the company's new offerings.


**Soundbite:** *"AWS services that launched in 2006 still work today. Google IoT Core? Gone. Firebase Dynamic Links? Gone. You're betting your architecture on a platform — pick one that doesn't retire services on you."*

### 🟡 WHERE IT IS A FIGHT

**1. AI/ML infrastructure and custom silicon**

Google has Ironwood TPUs; AWS has Trainium2 (currently deployed at scale) and Trainium3 in development. 
Ironwood offers a 10x peak performance improvement over TPU v5p and more than 4x better performance per chip for both training and inference compared to the previous generation.
 However, 
Anthropic trains and runs Claude on a range of AI hardware — AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, and Nvidia GPUs — matching workloads to the chips best suited for them.


**What tips it:** Customers who want NVIDIA-compatible paths, ecosystem breadth, and the primary AI lab relationships will lean AWS. Customers who want inference-optimized, cost-competitive TPU workloads may lean Google.

**2. Enterprise agentic AI platforms**

Google's Gemini Enterprise and AWS's Bedrock AgentCore were both launched in 2025 and are in direct competition. 
Gemini Enterprise saw 40% quarter-on-quarter growth in paid monthly active users in Q1 2026.
 **What tips it:** Enterprise accounts already deep in Google Workspace will be easier for Google to convert. Accounts with existing AWS data infrastructure (S3, Redshift, Lambda) should be protected by demonstrating the cost and complexity of moving data pipelines.

---

### 🔴 WHERE GOOGLE BEATS AWS

**1. Serverless analytics — BigQuery is genuinely superior for analytics-only workloads**

BigQuery's serverless model requires no cluster management, no vacuuming, and no indexing — it runs SQL directly on petabyte-scale datasets with zero ops overhead. This is a genuine gap AWS must acknowledge. Counter: Redshift Serverless now handles auto-scaling without cluster management, and Athena lets you query S3 data in place with no migration. The TCO advantage for BigQuery disappears when you factor in data migration costs from AWS-native services.

**Counter soundbite for AWS reps:** *"BigQuery is excellent for analytics-only workloads. But if your data lives in Aurora, DynamoDB, and S3, moving it to BigQuery adds latency and cost. Redshift Serverless runs on your data, where it is."*

**2. Kubernetes and container-native workloads**

Google invented Kubernetes. GKE remains the reference implementation, and developers who are Kubernetes-native often prefer GKE's tighter integrations and lower overhead compared to AWS EKS.

**3. Network performance for global, latency-sensitive applications**

Google Cloud runs on Google's private global fiber network — the same infrastructure that powers Search, YouTube, and Gmail — delivering measurably lower inter-region latency compared to networks that rely more heavily on public internet peering.

**4. Wiz acquisition — multicloud security narrative**

Until AWS can fully neutralize the Wiz/Google story, Google has a credible answer for the question "who secures my multicloud environment?" AWS's native stack is the correct counter (see Objection Handling below), but reps must be prepared to lead with it.

## Objection Handling

### Objection 1: "Google has better AI — Gemini is the most advanced model available."

**The real objection:** The prospect has seen Google Cloud Next demos or Gemini benchmark scores and believes Google has a model quality edge.

**AWS response:** Acknowledge the real strength — Google has a formidable model story. Then pivot to ecosystem and safety:


Amazon Bedrock gives access to hundreds of foundation models from leading AI companies, along with evaluation tools to pick the best model based on unique performance and cost needs.
 AWS's bet is that no single model wins every task — enterprises who lock into one model family trade flexibility for the illusion of simplicity. And on production scale: 
over 100,000 customers run Anthropic Claude models on AWS
 — that production depth matters more than benchmark scores. Additionally, 
Amazon remains Anthropic's primary cloud provider and training partner
, and OpenAI committed \$100B+ to AWS in 2026.

**Sharpest line:** *"Google wants you to run Gemini on Google Cloud. We want you to run whichever model is best for your workload — on the infrastructure where your data already lives, with the model families that matter most to your team."*

---

### Objection 2: "Google Cloud is cheaper — especially for analytics."

**The real objection:** The prospect has been shown a BigQuery pricing demo that looks significantly cheaper than Redshift, or seen Google's egress pricing.

**AWS response:** BigQuery's serverless model is genuinely simpler for analytics-only workloads. But total cost of ownership includes migration. Moving data from AWS-native services (Aurora, DynamoDB, S3) to BigQuery introduces real ingestion costs, latency, and a new vendor dependency that offset per-query savings. Counter with Redshift Serverless (no cluster management) and Athena (query S3 in place — no migration required).

**Sharpest line:** *"The cheapest analytics platform is the one where your data already lives. Moving petabytes to BigQuery costs money, time, and introduces a new vendor dependency. Redshift Serverless runs on your data, where it is."*

---

### Objection 3: "Google acquired Wiz — they have the best security story now, and it works across any cloud."

**The real objection:** The prospect already uses Wiz for multicloud security, trusts it, and sees Google's acquisition as legitimizing Google Cloud as the security platform of record.

**AWS response:** Acknowledge Wiz's strength — 
Wiz is trusted by 50% of the Fortune 100.
 It earned that reputation as an independent, multicloud-neutral tool. Now raise the strategic risk: 
this acquisition represents a significant investment by Google Cloud to accelerate improved cloud security and customers' ability to use multiple clouds.
 Wiz's roadmap is now owned by a direct competitor to AWS. Enterprises using Wiz in AWS environments are giving Google visibility into their AWS architecture, deployment patterns, and security posture.

For security-sensitive enterprises in financial services, defense, or healthcare: does your procurement policy allow a competitor cloud provider to serve as your primary security monitoring vendor inside your production infrastructure?

Counter with AWS's native security stack: 
Guardrails, IAM integration, VPC endpoints, and compliance certifications (FedRAMP High, HIPAA, SOC) satisfy enterprise security requirements
 natively — without a third-party vendor whose roadmap is now controlled by a competitor.

**Sharpest line:** *"You trusted Wiz because it was independent. It isn't anymore. Do you want Google reading your AWS security posture?"*
