# Competitive Intelligence Brief: Anthropic vs. OpenAI
---

## Executive Summary

**1. Anthropic's run-rate revenue has overtaken OpenAI — and the growth rate has no precedent in enterprise software history.**


Anthropic's revenue trajectory: \$87 million run rate in January 2024, \$1 billion by December 2024, \$9 billion by end of 2025, \$14 billion in February 2026, \$19 billion in March, and \$30 billion in April.
 
Anthropic has pulled ahead on reported run-rate revenue, with Anthropic at approximately \$45B ARR versus OpenAI's approximately \$33B run rate as of May 2026, per Sacra.
 Note: 
Anthropic reports revenue from cloud resellers (AWS, Google, Microsoft) on a gross basis — counting total end-customer spend as revenue and booking partner payouts as expenses — which inflates top-line figures relative to net-reporting peers.
 The growth rate is not in dispute. The absolute number should be weighted accordingly.

**So what:** Anthropic is the fastest-growing enterprise software vendor in recorded history by run-rate growth. Use this narrative aggressively in enterprise sales to signal momentum, durability, and the institutional shift of spend from OpenAI to Claude — but be precise about the run-rate methodology when talking to sophisticated CFOs.

---

**2. Claude leads the category that is eating enterprise AI spend: coding.**


Anthropic's ascent has been driven by its remarkably durable dominance in the coding market, where it now commands an estimated 54% market share, compared to 21% for OpenAI — up from 42% just six months ago, driven in large part by the popularity of Claude Code.
 
Coding is now 51% of all enterprise generative AI usage — by a wide margin, the highest-value use case in the market.
 On the benchmark that matters: 
Claude Opus 4.8 scores 88.6% on SWE-bench Verified
, and 
on SWE-bench Pro — the harder, contamination-resistant variant — Opus 4.8 lands at 69.2%, over 10 points ahead of GPT-5.5 (58.6%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (54.2%).


**So what:** Claude Code's dominance in the highest-growth enterprise use case is the primary commercial lever right now. Sales motions targeting developer-led organizations should anchor on coding benchmarks and Claude Code vs. Codex differentiation specifically. Every engineering-heavy enterprise account is a target this quarter.

---

**3. OpenAI is a mass-market giant running hot on borrowed money — and it is now charging into Anthropic's territory.**


OpenAI is generating \$2B in revenue per month.
 
Enterprise now makes up more than 40% of OpenAI's revenue and is on track to reach parity with consumer by the end of 2026.
 But: 
projected cash burn has risen to approximately \$27B in 2026 and approximately \$63B in 2027; OpenAI does not turn cash-flow positive until 2030.


**So what:** OpenAI is explicitly charging into Anthropic's primary territory. The window to capture strategic enterprise accounts before OpenAI fully arrives is now, not next year. Accelerate named-account sales motions and make switching costs explicit. Simultaneously, monitor OpenAI pricing changes as an early signal of financial distress — a company burning \$27B/year on the road to an IPO has structural pressure to raise prices.

---

**4. OpenAI's Stargate infrastructure bet has materially rescoped — and the instability creates a real vendor-risk argument.**


In early 2025, OpenAI announced Stargate, a joint venture with Oracle and SoftBank aiming to invest \$500 billion in AI data centers. But after more than a year of challenges and disagreements, OpenAI has abandoned the original idea of directly owning infrastructure and now prefers to rely on third-party providers and lease capacity in the long term.
 
In the space of one week, OpenAI pulled out of two European Stargate data center deals, one in the UK and the second in Norway.
 
Its astronomical commitments to spending \$1.4 trillion before the end of the decade on AI infrastructure was recently more than halved to a still-hefty \$600 billion.


**So what:** OpenAI's infrastructure story has gone from "building the world's largest AI supercomputer" to "leasing compute like everyone else." This is a real, usable vendor-risk argument in enterprise sales. Meanwhile, 
Claude remains the only frontier AI model available to customers on all three of the world's largest cloud platforms: Amazon Web Services (Bedrock), Google Cloud (Vertex AI), and Microsoft Azure (Foundry)
 — a multi-cloud optionality story OpenAI cannot match.

---

**5. OpenAI's IPO path introduces post-IPO pricing risk for every enterprise customer on their platform.**


OpenAI is preparing to confidentially file a draft of its IPO prospectus, working with banks including Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley to file in the coming days or weeks.
 
The company is targeting a public market debut in September 2026.
 
Once OpenAI lists, it will face quarterly pressure to grow revenue and demonstrate margin improvement — creating incentives to either raise prices, expand the product portfolio, or both.


**So what:** Any enterprise currently negotiating OpenAI contracts should be told explicitly: Q3 2026 may be the last window before shareholder demands reshape their pricing playbook. For Anthropic sales, this is an opening to lock in multi-year enterprise contracts now, while OpenAI is distracted by its IPO process and before public-market pressure pushes their prices up.

## Snapshot: OpenAI

**What they do:** OpenAI builds frontier AI models and deploys them through ChatGPT (consumer), ChatGPT Enterprise, and the OpenAI API. 
OpenAI's stated strategy: "Our superapp will bring together ChatGPT, Codex, browsing, and our broader agentic capabilities into one agent-first experience."
 Consumer familiarity is the explicit wedge into enterprise procurement.

**Size and scale:** 
ChatGPT has more than 900 million weekly active users and over 50 million subscribers.
 
APIs now process more than 15 billion tokens per minute.
 
The business user base stood at more than 9M paying business users as of February 2026.


**Funding and valuation:** 
On March 31, 2026, OpenAI closed a \$122B funding round at an \$852B post-money valuation, co-led by SoftBank alongside Andreessen Horowitz, D.E. Shaw Ventures, MGX, TPG, and T. Rowe Price-advised accounts.


**Revenue trajectory:** 
OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar confirmed \$20B in 2025 revenue alongside \$6B in 2024 and \$2B in 2023, reflecting roughly 10x growth from 2023 to 2025.
 Note: Sacra's annualized figures exclude Microsoft licensing revenue and large one-time deals, focusing on core subscription and API business.

**Governance:** 
In October 2025, OpenAI completed a recapitalization converting the for-profit subsidiary into OpenAI Group PBC while keeping the nonprofit — renamed the OpenAI Foundation — in control.

## Recent Strategic Moves

**Stargate (January 2025 – ongoing):** 
Stargate Project is an American multinational AI joint venture created by OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle, and MGX, planning to invest up to \$500 billion in AI infrastructure in the United States by 2029.
 In practice, 
after more than a year of challenges and disagreements, OpenAI has abandoned the original idea of directly owning infrastructure and now prefers to rely on third-party providers and lease capacity in the long term.
 **Signal:** Stargate is real but has been rescoped from self-built ownership to a leasing-from-partners model; compute uncertainty remains a live risk.

**Codex (2025 – ongoing):** 
Codex now serves over 2 million weekly users, up 5x in the past three months, with usage growing more than 70% month over month.
 This is OpenAI's direct answer to Claude Code. **Signal:** The coding agent race is a two-horse competition between Codex and Claude Code — and both are in hypergrowth.

**ChatGPT Ads (February 2026 – ongoing):** 
OpenAI officially launched ads in ChatGPT on February 9, 2026, for Free and Go tier users in the United States. The ad pilot surpassed \$100 million in annualized revenue in under 60 days.
 
OpenAI is projecting \$2.5 billion in ChatGPT ad revenue this year.
 
The ad pilot has expanded to the UK, Mexico, Brazil, Japan, and South Korea.
 **Signal:** OpenAI is building a third revenue leg beyond subscriptions and API. Anthropic has no equivalent ad business. Long-term, this could fund model improvements that change the competitive cost dynamic.

**IPO (May 2026 – ongoing):** 
OpenAI confidentially filed its S-1 IPO prospectus with the SEC on Friday May 22, 2026, targeting a Q4 2026 public listing at a valuation between \$852 billion and \$1 trillion. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the deal.
 **Signal:** The IPO process will force financial transparency and create post-IPO pricing pressure on every enterprise contract.

## Positioning and Differentiation

**Consumer flywheel as enterprise wedge:** 
Enterprise now makes up more than 40% of OpenAI's revenue and is on track to reach parity with consumer by the end of 2026.
 The thesis: consumer familiarity with ChatGPT converts to enterprise procurement.

**Multimodal completeness:** OpenAI has a broader multimodal stack including image generation, voice, and video capabilities. Claude handles text and image inputs but does not generate images, video, or audio. OpenAI explicitly positions this breadth as the "buy once" enterprise option.

**Ads as structural moat:** 
OpenAI projects \$2.5 billion in ad revenue for full-year 2026, growing to \$53 billion by 2029 and \$100 billion by 2030.
 If even a fraction of that materializes, it creates a cross-subsidy engine for model development that Anthropic cannot replicate without an equivalent consumer base.

### ⚔️ WHERE IT IS A FIGHT

**General enterprise AI, agentic workflows, developer tooling**

Both Codex and Claude Code are in hypergrowth. 
Since May 2025, Anthropic's enterprise penetration jumped 25 percentage points to 44% — the largest gain of any AI provider in that period.
 
The April 2026 Ramp data showing Anthropic at 34.4% vs. OpenAI at 32.3% is the first time Anthropic has led in business spending share in Ramp's dataset — based on actual card and invoice transactions, not surveys.


What tips the fight toward Anthropic: context window advantage (1M tokens vs. GPT-5.4's ~1M comparable, with Claude's multi-cloud availability), existing Fortune 10 relationships, and the Menlo Ventures coding share lead. What tips it toward OpenAI: existing ChatGPT Enterprise contracts at Fortune 500 companies, Microsoft/Azure integration advantage, and Codex's accelerating growth.

**Soundbite:** *"Both Codex and Claude Code are growing at unprecedented rates. The question is which you can build deeper workflows on without hitting context walls or re-prompting after every model update. Claude's production stability, context depth, and 54% coding market share are what differentiate here."*

### ❌ WHERE OPENAI BEATS ANTHROPIC

**Consumer mindshare, multimodal richness, and SMB/broad-productivity buyers**


ChatGPT has more than 900 million weekly active users, 6x the monthly web visits and mobile sessions of the next largest AI app, and 4x all AI time spent combined.
 That consumer scale is a genuine distribution moat into the enterprise.

OpenAI's multimodal stack — image generation, voice, and the ads platform — gives it capabilities Anthropic does not yet have. 
Enterprises building customer service bots that need to process image uploads, or internal tools that analyze charts and screenshots, often still reach for OpenAI's multimodal stack.


**Soundbite:** *"If your buyer needs image generation, voice, a plugin ecosystem, or a model that's already embedded in their Microsoft stack via Azure, OpenAI wins that conversation today. Anthropic is the right choice where the work is deep, the documents are long, and the code is real."*

## Sentiment

*Sources: Reddit, review forums, community sites. Treated as Tier 4 — valid for sentiment direction, never for hard facts.*

### OpenAI / ChatGPT Sentiment

**Complaints:** Community sentiment has been notably negative around perceived behavioral changes in ChatGPT — users on Reddit and developer forums report the model becoming more restrictive and less predictable, with complaints about safety override behaviors that activate without clear notice and cannot be disabled. Developers building on the API report frustration with output inconsistencies between model versions. The introduction of ads on Free and Go tiers has generated concern among some longtime subscribers about the direction of the platform.

**Praise:** ChatGPT's Deep Research mode, extensive plugin ecosystem, and broad multimodal capabilities continue to draw strong praise. Consumer ease of use and familiarity remain genuine assets.

### Anthropic / Claude Sentiment

**Praise:** Reddit consensus consistently awards Claude the edge for writing quality, complex instruction-following, and nuanced reasoning. Users specifically note Claude's tendency to acknowledge uncertainty rather than confabulate — a meaningful trust signal for enterprise use. Claude Code's ability to work autonomously on entire projects for extended periods is a recurring positive theme.

**Complaints:** 
Anthropic acknowledged that demand for Claude has led to "inevitable strain on our infrastructure," impacting "reliability and performance" for users, particularly during peak hours.
 Lack of image/video generation capability is a persistent complaint from users who want a single multimodal tool. Enterprise pricing opacity (no public rate card) slows procurement decisions at some organizations.

## Objection Handling

### Objection 1: "We're already standardized on ChatGPT Enterprise. Migrating would be disruptive."

**Response:** The "standardization" risk is real but overstated for engineering-heavy orgs. 
Anthropic commands an estimated 54% of the enterprise coding market, compared to 21% for OpenAI.
 Most sophisticated enterprise buyers are already running both — 
79% of companies paying for Anthropic are already paying for OpenAI too.
 The frame shouldn't be "migration" — it should be "adding a specialized coding and long-context layer to your existing stack." 
Claude is available on AWS Bedrock, Google Cloud Vertex AI, and Microsoft Azure Foundry
 — meaning Claude likely runs on the same cloud infrastructure your OpenAI workloads do already.

---

### Objection 2: "OpenAI has better models — they're shipping faster than anyone."

**Response:** OpenAI's cadence is real, but it creates a different problem: instability. Teams that built tight couplings to specific OpenAI model versions have spent significant engineering time on migrations. On the benchmark that matters most for enterprise software development, 
Claude Opus 4.8 leads SWE-bench Pro at 69.2%, over 10 points ahead of GPT-5.5 (58.6%).
 The honest answer: OpenAI leads on consumer multimodal and breadth; Claude leads on coding and long-context tasks. If your primary use case is enterprise software development or document-intensive workflows, Claude wins on the benchmarks directly applicable to your work.

---

### Objection 3: "OpenAI is the safe enterprise choice — they're the market leader with the most institutional backing."

**Response:** "Safe" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. 
Projected burn has risen to approximately \$27B in 2026 and approximately \$63B in 2027; OpenAI does not turn cash-flow positive until 2030.
 A company burning \$27B/year en route to an IPO has structural pressure to raise prices or reduce model quality — and post-IPO, quarterly earnings pressure intensifies that. 
Claude, by contrast, is the only frontier model available across all three major cloud platforms — AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure
 — delivering multi-cloud optionality without single-vendor lock-in. "Safe enterprise choice" should mean predictable behavior, durable vendor health, and multi-cloud flexibility — all of which favor Anthropic.
